01 / Logo

The Squirrel & the Hollow

The Time Squirrel mark is a single illustrated icon: a russet squirrel inside a tree hollow, holding a clock-faced acorn, with a small pile of acorns to the side. It is metaphor as identity — the hollow is local storage, the acorns are saved sessions, the clock is time being collected. The mark always appears as a complete unit; the squirrel is never separated from its hollow.

Logo Anatomy
Clearspace = 1/8 of icon width
Rounded square frame

The mark sits inside a rounded square with a corner radius equal to ~24% of the side length. This shape echoes the macOS app icon grid — Time Squirrel is a Mac-native product, and the icon should feel native at every scale from Dock to Spotlight.

The hollow

The dark archway behind the squirrel is the tree hollow — and conceptually, it is the user's local storage. The squirrel lives in there. The acorns live in there. The clock lives in there. Anyone modifying or recreating the mark should preserve the hollow as the visual frame for everything else.

The clock-acorn

The squirrel holds a small acorn whose face is a clock — the literal merging of "time" and "thing being collected." Even at small sizes, the clock face must remain readable. If clarity fails below 32px, fall back to the mark-only variant or a simplified glyph.

Wordmark, when paired

The wordmark is set in Fraunces 700, with "Squirrel" optionally rendered in italic for warmth. The wordmark sits below or to the right of the mark, never inside the hollow. Use the icon alone when space is tight; use icon + wordmark for marketing surfaces.

Logo Rules
✓ Correct Use

Use the mark as a complete unit. Maintain clearspace equal to 1/8 of icon width on all sides. The icon may be reproduced in approved color variants only. For app distribution, always use the highest-resolution PNG export available.

✕ Incorrect Use

Don't extract the squirrel from the hollow. Don't recolor with off-palette tones. Don't add drop shadows, glows, or 3D effects. Don't stretch the rounded square into other proportions. Don't replace the squirrel with another animal "for fun."

02 / Color

A warm den, not a workspace

03 / Typography

Warm serif, quiet sans

Time Squirrel uses Fraunces — a contemporary, optically-sized serif with soft contours — for display. It signals craft, warmth, and a small-tool sensibility. Body copy is set in Inter for clarity and density. Code is JetBrains Mono. The combination is honest and grown-up, not playful or office-bland.

Callouts

✦
Single-session model

Time Squirrel runs at most one session at a time. To start a new one, stop or reset the current session. Laps are sub-segments of that single session, not concurrent timers.

⚠
Sleep behavior — timer mode

Timers always pause on sleep and resume on wake. This is not configurable, by design. Continuing a countdown across sleep would silently complete the timer without notifying you.

✕
Reset is destructive

Reset discards the current session without saving. There is no undo. Use Stop to save the session to history; use Reset only when you want the session not to exist.

06 / Usage Guidelines

What Time Squirrel Is Not

Positioning matters. Time Squirrel sits in a crowded category — every productivity-tracker SaaS in the world wants to be your timer. These guidelines keep the brand honest and prevent it from drifting into territory it explicitly does not want to occupy.

✓ Time Squirrel is

A native macOS app for capturing one session at a time. A small focus tool for individuals. A producer of human-readable Markdown records you actually own. An open-source tool you can fork and modify. A quiet companion in your menu bar.

✕ Time Squirrel is not

A project management tool. A team time-tracker. A billing or invoicing system. A multi-timer dashboard. A cloud-synced service. A SaaS product. A "productivity platform." A replacement for thinking carefully about how you spend your time.

Brand Context Rules
Product name

Always "Time Squirrel" — two words, both capitalized. Never "TimeSquirrel," "timesquirrel," or "TS." The full name is short enough to spell out everywhere.

Tagline

"Collect your time. Keep it local." Two sentences, period after each, no Oxford-style flourishes. The tagline is the product spec compressed into seven words.

Mascot reference

"The squirrel" is acceptable shorthand. Avoid anthropomorphizing further — no "she," no name, no backstory. The squirrel is a metaphor for the product, not a brand character.

07 / Voice & Tone

Quiet, warm, precise

Time Squirrel is built by one person, for one person first. The voice should feel like that — a small workshop, not a corporate platform. Honest about what it is and isn't. Warm without being cute. Calm without being cold.

Plain

Use ordinary words. Time Squirrel doesn't "leverage" or "empower" — it saves a file. Plain language earns trust.

"Sessions save as Markdown." — not "Captured time intervals are persisted in human-readable format."

Honest

Say what the product does and doesn't do. Time Squirrel doesn't sync to the cloud. It doesn't have teams. Don't apologize for the absence — that's the product.

"No cloud, no accounts, no team features. By design." — not "Cloud sync coming soon!"

Warm

The squirrel metaphor allows light personality. Acorns get collected, the hollow holds your stash. Don't push it — but don't strip it out either.

"The squirrel keeps your acorns local." — acceptable. "The squirrel is excited to track your productivity!" — never.

Specific

Concrete details outperform abstract claims. Name the file, the format, the path, the duration. Specificity is reassurance.

"Saved to ~/Documents/TimeSquirrel/2026-04-26-customer-call.md" — not "Your session has been saved."

Calm

Time Squirrel is a quiet tool. Copy doesn't shout. No exclamation marks in product UI. No urgency where there is none.

"Session ended. 47 minutes." — not "Great work! You crushed that 47-minute session!"

Frank

If a feature isn't there, say so. If something is by design, say so. Don't hedge with "currently" or "for now" unless that is genuinely accurate.

"Time Squirrel runs one session at a time. By design." — not "Multi-session support is on the roadmap."
Writing Rules
Do
  • Use plain words. "Save," "stop," "reset" — not "persist," "halt," "reinitialize."
  • Name file paths and formats specifically when relevant
  • Lead with what the user gets, then explain how
  • Use second person ("your time," "your machine") — Time Squirrel works for the user, not the brand
  • Keep marketing copy as short as the product is small
  • Acknowledge tradeoffs — "Single session is a feature, not a limitation"
  • Write so a fork could happen tomorrow without changing the product story
Don't
  • Use SaaS verbs — "leverage," "empower," "streamline," "supercharge"
  • Promise what isn't delivered ("seamless," "magical," "AI-powered")
  • Add exclamation marks to product UI — ever
  • Anthropomorphize the squirrel into a character
  • Say "we" in product documentation — Time Squirrel is a tool, not a team
  • Hedge core constraints with "currently" or "for now"
  • Compare directly to other timer products — Time Squirrel sits beside them, not against them
